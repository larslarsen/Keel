Keel

Keel records the recommendation surfaces shown to you on YouTube and supported TikTok feeds, so you can inspect and control them. The raw recording stays on this computer.

What it records

On YouTube watch pages and the homepage: the offered video's title, channel, length, view count and position. Each watch-page recommendation is recorded against the video you had open, so the file also contains a list of videos you opened on those pages. On supported TikTok feeds, Keel records the rendered clips in scroll order: their id, caption, creator, hashtags and sound when present.

What it does not record

Not how you watch — whether you pressed play, how long you stayed, or whether you finished. Not what you search for. Not who you are: there is no account and no sign-in. Nothing outside the named youtube.com and tiktok.com surfaces.

Where it goes

Into a file on this computer, written by the Keel desktop app you install yourself. The raw rows and ordered trail are not sent to us — there is no Keel server to send them to. You can export everything as JSON, or delete all of it, at any time.

Nothing is recorded until you press that. Recording is Keel — its suggestions, search and analysis are all computed from what it has kept here — so until you do, it has nothing to show you. If you change your mind later, remove the extension or delete the desktop app; the Your data page also erases everything it holds, permanently.

Publishing recommendation blocks is separate, and off by default. At every level Keel still participates in the peer network: it requests broad shared data, announces livestreams it sees, and exchanges a whole fixed-shape word-popularity aggregate that includes its local corpus. The word pack carries no plaintext words, video ids, edges or search query, but known-word guesses can be tested against it. Choosing "broad sharing" additionally sends counts derived from your recording: which video appeared alongside which, grouped by rough position and day, with no raw timestamp, title, query, page visit or watch order. Your computer serves each complete broad bucket with both its own claims and cached peer claims, without labelling which is which. Different neighbourhoods use unlinkable claim keys, but peers still see ordinary connection metadata and may link deliveries by address or timing.

A livestream notice carries the video and no application-level sender — not your name or an identifier for your copy of Keel. A directly connected peer still sees your network address and may infer an origin from topology and timing.
