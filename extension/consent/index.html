Keel

Keel records the recommendation surfaces shown to you on YouTube and supported TikTok feeds, so you can inspect and control them. The raw recording stays on this computer.

What it records

On YouTube watch pages and the homepage: the offered video's title, channel, length, view count and position. Each watch-page recommendation is recorded against the video you had open, so the file also contains a list of videos you opened on those pages. On supported TikTok feeds, Keel records the rendered clips in scroll order: their id, caption, creator, hashtags and sound when present.

What it does not record

Not how you watch — whether you pressed play, how long you stayed, or whether you finished. Not what you search for. Not who you are: there is no account and no sign-in. Nothing outside the named youtube.com and tiktok.com surfaces.

Where it goes

Into a file on this computer, written by the Keel desktop app you install yourself. The raw rows and ordered trail are not sent to us — there is no Keel server to send them to. You can export everything as JSON, or delete all of it, at any time.

What it downloads

Keel joins a peer-to-peer network and downloads three things: a starter recommendation dataset, groups of shared recommendation data that make its suggestions work, and a global word statistic used for the bars under the search box. Asking for those is a request other computers can see — they learn your address, the time, and roughly which slice of the catalogue you asked about, never the video you wanted. There is no Keel server; the answers come from other people running Keel.

Nothing derived from what you were shown is sent. Not a recommendation, not a livestream you saw, not a word from your own library. Sharing any of that is a separate choice called Broad sharing, on the Your data page, and it is off unless you turn it on.

Nothing is recorded and nothing is downloaded until you press that, and the desktop app enforces the second part itself: it will not open a network connection until it has your answer, whatever this browser thinks. Recording is Keel — its suggestions, search and analysis are all computed from what it has kept here — so until you do, it has nothing to show you. If you change your mind later, remove the extension or delete the desktop app; the Your data page also erases everything it holds, permanently.

Sharing what you were shown is separate, and off by default. Choosing Broad sharing on the Your data page is the decision that publishes livestreams you saw, contributes your word-popularity aggregate, answers other people's searches, and serves complete broad buckets containing both cached peer claims and aggregated recommendation counts derived from your recording. A livestream notice carries no sender field, but a peer you are connected to can still infer that it started with you from timing. The word pack carries no plaintext words, video ids, edges or search query, but a guessed word can be tested against it. Recommendation counts are grouped by rough position and day, with no raw timestamp, title, query, page visit or watch order, and are sent only as whole buckets of thousands of videos.
